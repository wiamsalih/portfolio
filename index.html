04 / Research

Questions I keep coming back to.

Bridging Expertise Gaps: A Comparative Study of AI Accessibility Across User Populations

Author Wiam SalihInstitution Cornell Tech / prior GMU researchDraft January 2026
Ongoing inquiry
Thesis

AI systems often assume a generic user. My research asks what changes when the user is an expert versus a novice — and how interface design can preserve both comprehension and agency. The work connects Skylar's expert workflow with FinBot's general-audience experience.

Question 01
How should AI adapt to expertise?
Experts often need efficient access to detailed evidence; novices need explanations that help them build a mental model.
Question 02
What creates trustworthy interaction?
Trust is shaped not only by accuracy, but by whether people understand what the AI is doing and feel able to disagree.
Question 03
What should stay human?
The goal is not maximum automation. It is deciding which parts of a workflow AI can accelerate without removing meaningful user control.
Preliminary findings
01
Trust is about agency, not accuracy. Early observations suggest users care about whether they can challenge or disagree with the system, not simply whether it produces a correct answer.
02
Accessibility changes with expertise. Experts benefit from dense diagnostic evidence; novices benefit from explanations that build foundational understanding.
03
Expertise should shape interaction. Vocabulary, information density, transparency, and follow-up behavior should be calibrated to the user's background and task.
